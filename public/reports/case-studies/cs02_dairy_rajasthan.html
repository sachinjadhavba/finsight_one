02 The Problem
Challenge 1: Projections Labelled "Over-Optimistic" by Bank

The client's CA had projected 35% revenue growth in Year 1 after the UHT line comes online. The bank's credit officer marked this as aggressive and unsubstantiated. In reality, the UHT line would add a new SKU (12-month shelf life) that could sell in modern retail chains — a fundamentally different revenue stream that the previous format was failing to distinguish from the existing fresh-milk business.

Challenge 2: NABARD DEDS Subsidy Not Claimed — Inflated Loan Requirement

Under NABARD's Dairy Entrepreneurship Development Scheme (DEDS), dairy processing units are eligible for a capital subsidy of 25% of project cost (maximum ₹40 lakh for certain components). The client and their CA were unaware of this scheme. The loan was being applied at full project cost of ₹18.5 Crore, making DSCR borderline. With DEDS subsidy correctly factored in, the net loan requirement reduces to ₹14.5 Crore — dramatically improving all coverage ratios.

Challenge 3: Cold Chain Assets Undervalued in Collateral

The client had 12 refrigerated transport vehicles and a 200 MT cold storage facility. These were listed as zero in the collateral schedule because the bank's valuers do not typically value operational assets in this sector. However, the cold chain equipment had a market value of approximately ₹2.2 Crore — which, when added to the plant mortgage, was sufficient to close the collateral gap.

03 What FinsightOne Did
Action 1: Split the Revenue Projections into Two Business Segments

We restructured the projections to show two separate P&L streams: (a) Existing business (fresh milk, dahi, paneer) — conservative flat growth at 8% CAGR aligned with historical performance. (b) New UHT business — phased ramp-up with Year 1 at 40% utilisation, Year 2 at 65%, Year 3 at 85%, backed by letters of intent from two Rajasthan retail chains for shelf space. This made the growth story factual and traceable, not optimistic.

Action 2: Applied NABARD DEDS Subsidy — Reduced Loan by ₹4 Crore

We filed the DEDS application on behalf of the client, which was accepted. The ₹40 lakh + ₹3.6 Crore back-ended subsidy component reduced effective project cost from ₹18.5 Crore to ₹14.5 Crore. We restructured the loan application around this revised amount, improving DSCR from 1.11x to 1.44x — well within the bank's threshold.

Action 3: Got Cold Chain Assets Independently Valued

We arranged an IRDAI-approved independent valuation of the cold chain equipment and vehicles. This added ₹2.2 Crore to the collateral pool. Combined with the plant mortgage and DEDS subsidy reducing loan amount, collateral coverage moved from 72% to 116%.

The Analogy That Helped: The bank was looking at the growth projection like it was measuring a single tree. We showed them the orchard — the UHT line is an entirely new orchard planted next to the existing one. A 35% jump in revenue isn't a stretch; it's what happens when you add a new product category with 12-month shelf life to a fresh-milk business with only 3-day shelf life. Two completely different markets.

02 Why the File Was Stuck — The Problem
Challenge 1: The CMA Data Was Not Sugar-Specific

The borrower's accountant had prepared CMA Data in a generic manufacturing format. Sugar mills have a highly seasonal working capital cycle — crushing runs for only 5–6 months (October to March). The CMA data was presenting year-round uniform drawdowns, which misrepresented the actual cash flow pattern and alarmed the bank's credit officer about perpetual CC utilisation.

Challenge 2: PPA Revenue Was Excluded from Cash Flow Projections

The co-gen plant generates steady, year-round cash flows under the MSEDCL PPA (Power Purchase Agreement) — ₹28.4 Crore annually, received as monthly tariff payments. This revenue had been parked under "other income" in the CA's projections and excluded from the main debt service calculation. This significantly depressed the computed DSCR.

Challenge 3: Consortium Arrangement Not Structured Correctly

The client had approached two banks independently with overlapping proposals, creating confusion about lead bank, charge structure, and pro-rata sharing. Neither bank wanted to move first. The file had been in limbo between two credit departments for 8 months.

03 What FinsightOne Did
Action 1: Rebuilt CMA Data on Seasonal Cash Flow Model

We redesigned the CMA Data using a month-wise drawing power statement that mirrors the sugar mill's actual crushing season. Peak CC utilisation shown only during October–March crushing season; rapid repayment in April–June from sugar sale proceeds. This is standard for sugar mill credit — and the pattern aligned with the bank's own internal sugar sector circular (NABARD Refinancing Guidelines for Cooperative Sugar Factories).

Action 2: Integrated PPA Revenue into Debt Service Computation

The MSEDCL PPA tariff payments are legally "scheduled receivables" — guaranteed and foreseeable cash flows backed by a state government utility. We documented this with a copy of the 25-year PPA, MSEDCL payment history for 36 months (100% on-time), and a banker's perspective note citing RBI's Renewable Energy / Co-gen Power Project guidelines. Including this income increased the DSCR from an apparent 0.94x to a bankable 1.48x.

Action 3: Structured Proper Consortium with Lead Bank Agreement

We identified a third PSU bank as lead bank with appetite for agri-processing credit. Structured a consortium of three banks: Lead Bank (WC: ₹28 Cr), Member Bank 1 (WC: ₹14 Cr), Member Bank 2 (TL: ₹18 Cr). Prepared the Inter-Se Agreement format and charge document structure. Presented a single unified credit proposal to all three banks simultaneously, with each bank's portion clearly carved out.

The Analogy That Helped: We explained the seasonal CC to the bank's credit officer like this — "A sugar mill's cash cycle is like a camel. It works hard for 5 months, loads up on water (draws CC), then lives off the hump for the next 7 months (repays from sugar sales). You wouldn't penalise a camel for not drinking every day." The officer had never seen the seasonal model visualised this clearly — it changed the conversation.

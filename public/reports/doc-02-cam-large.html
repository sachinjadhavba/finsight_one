01 EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
This Loan Appraisal Note has been prepared by FinsightOne on behalf of the borrower to present a structured case for a Term Loan of Rs. 3.50 Crore for machinery acquisition and factory expansion. The borrower is a Private Limited company engaged in readymade garment manufacturing and export at Tiruppur, Tamil Nadu — the acknowledged hosiery and knitwear capital of India. The company has been in operations for 11 years and exports to buyers in the EU and Middle East.
The proposed facility will fund the purchase of 24 automated knitting machines, 6 computerised embroidery units, and civil work for an additional 4,000 sq.ft. production floor — enabling capacity expansion from 1.2 lakh pieces/month to 2.4 lakh pieces/month. The expansion is fully backed by confirmed export orders from 3 overseas buyers with Letters of Intent enclosed, providing revenue visibility for the first 18 months post-expansion.
The company presents a clean credit profile — Directors' CIBIL scores of 762 and 748, consistent export turnover growth at 16% CAGR over three years, and ITR, GST, and banking data fully aligned. The security package — primary hypothecation of funded machinery and collateral mortgage of factory premises valued at Rs. 5.60 Crore — provides combined coverage of 276% of the proposed loan, offering strong recovery comfort to the lending institution.

04 INDUSTRY & MARKET CONTEXT
Tiruppur contributes approximately 90% of India's total knitwear exports and is a globally recognised apparel manufacturing hub. The cluster benefits from decades of accumulated supply chain depth — yarn suppliers, dyeing units, finishing facilities, and export agents all within a 30 km radius, keeping costs competitive relative to other manufacturing geographies. India's garment exports have benefited from the China+1 diversification strategy adopted by European and US buyers post-2020, and Tiruppur units with established buyer relationships are direct beneficiaries.
The borrower operates in the higher-margin branded and private-label segment (polo shirts, premium t-shirts) for EU buyers — these are not commodity garments. EU buyers typically require compliance with OEKO-TEX, GOTS, or equivalent certifications, which the borrower holds, creating a meaningful barrier to entry and reducing substitution risk from lower-cost competitors.
Key Risk — Buyer Concentration: Germany accounts for 42% of revenues. This is partially mitigated by the long-standing nature of the relationship (8 years with the primary German buyer) and the addition of 2 new LOI buyers in UAE and UK who collectively represent the next 18 months' incremental volume. Geographic diversification is a stated strategic priority for FY27.

Areas for Lender's Consideration
Buyer concentration — Germany 42%: Single country risk. Company is actively diversifying; UAE and UK LOI buyers represent deliberate geographic spread. New buyer mix reduces Germany share to ~35% post-expansion.
Import-dependent machinery (Mayer & Cie): Long lead time (16–20 weeks) and forex exposure on import invoice. Both are industry-standard; company has managed prior machinery imports without issue.
TOL/TNW rises to ~2.62x post-loan from current 0.84x — remains within 4x norm and will revert as loan is repaid. FY25 net worth of Rs. 1.78 Cr provides strong equity buffer.
6-month moratorium increases total interest cost — accepted by promoters as operationally necessary given machine installation timeline. Post-expansion cash flow comfortably absorbs EMI from Month 7 onwards.

10 PROPOSAL STRENGTHS — WHY THIS FILE STANDS OUT
The following factors, drawn directly from the borrower's operational record and verified documentation, highlight what makes this proposal stand out and are presented for the lending institution's consideration.
🏦
Exemplary Banking Conduct — 7 Years Clean, Growing Inflows
Current account credits have grown from Rs. 4.35 Cr/month (FY23) to Rs. 5.85 Cr/month (FY25) — a consistent, documented upward trend. The existing Export Packing Credit of Rs. 80 Lakhs has been renewed multiple times without overdue. Zero cheque bounces, zero CC excesses on record. Banking turnover at 2.01x of proposed loan in FY25 — income fully traceable through banking system.
🌍
Export Credentials Fully Verified — DGFT, FEMA, and AD Bank Records
Export turnover is corroborated across three independent sources: DGFT shipping bills, FEMA return submissions, and AD bank inward remittance records — all consistent with ITR and GST filings. This triple-source verification eliminates the income documentation risk that lenders frequently encounter with export-oriented borrowers. GST GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are current for all 36 months with zero pending returns.
📦
Demand-Backed Expansion — Rs. 8.40 Crore Confirmed Order Book
This is not a speculative capacity build. Three overseas buyers have issued Letters of Intent totalling Rs. 8.40 Crore for delivery within 18 months post-expansion. The primary German buyer has a continuous relationship of 8 years with no year of zero orders. The LOI value alone is 2.4x the proposed loan — indicating that the expansion is essentially self-funded through the revenue it unlocks.
🏛️
Strong Collateral — Factory Property at 160% LTV Coverage
The SIDCO Industrial Estate factory property — industrial NA use, clear title, 32 years remaining on SIDCO lease — is independently valued at Rs. 5.60 Crore. This single collateral covers 160% of the proposed loan without counting the primary hypothecation. SIDCO industrial land in Tiruppur has demonstrated consistent value appreciation given the cluster's export-driven demand. Title and EC are clean and enclosed.
📈
Rapid Deleveraging — Net Worth More Than Doubled in 3 Years
Net worth has grown from Rs. 82.4L (FY23) to Rs. 178.2L (FY25) — a 116% increase in 3 years — driven entirely by profit retention. The company has paid no dividends, reinvesting cash flows into the business. This discipline has driven TOL/TNW from 1.51x to 0.84x over the same period — one of the strongest deleveraging trajectories in this loan size category.
🎖️
Quality Certifications Create a Defensible Market Position
The company holds OEKO-TEX Standard 100 and GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard) certifications — mandatory for supply to most EU retail chains. These certifications take 12–18 months and significant investment to obtain, and require annual renewal audits. They represent a meaningful barrier to substitution: EU buyers cannot simply switch to an uncertified supplier. This structural moat underpins the long-term stability of the borrower's revenue base.

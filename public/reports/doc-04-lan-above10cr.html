Jaipur as a Global Jewellery Hub: Jaipur is the world's largest centre for coloured gemstone cutting and processing, and one of India's top 3 export jewellery manufacturing locations. Jaipur units — particularly those specialising in gemstone-studded designs — serve a global B2B market that has limited alternative supply bases outside India.

B2B Export Model — Lower Risk than Retail: The borrower operates exclusively in the B2B wholesale export segment — no retail, no domestic credit sales. This model eliminates consumer credit risk, retail inventory build-up risk, and fashion cycle risk. B2B buyers place orders with specific design and delivery specifications; production is against confirmed purchase orders, not speculative inventory build. SWIFT payment terms ensure advance receipt before shipment, providing strong working capital predictability.

On Thin Margins — The Correct Interpretation: PAT margins of 3–4% in export jewellery are a sector characteristic, not a business weakness. The entire industry operates on high-value material (gold, gemstones) with low value-addition as a percentage of FOB value. The correct profitability metric is EBITDA on value-added, not PAT on gross FOB value. The borrower's EBITDA on value-added (manufacturing spread) is 28–32% — strong by any manufacturing standard. A lender who measures jewellery profitability purely on PAT/Sales will systematically misread every good jewellery file.

On Gold Metal Loan Structure: A GML is fundamentally different from a cash WC loan against gold inventory. The bank provides gold metal (not cash), the borrower manufactures and exports, and repays the bank in gold or equivalent on export realisation. 07 GOLD PRICE RISK — STRUCTURE & HEDGE
How the GML mechanism eliminates gold price risk: Under the Gold Metal Loan structure, the borrower receives gold from the bank at the prevailing MCX/IBJA price on the date of disbursement. The borrower manufactures and exports. Upon export realisation, the borrower repays the bank in gold (or cash equivalent at prevailing price on repayment date). The borrower's manufacturing spread — the difference between the export value of finished jewellery and the cost of gold — is fixed by the export purchase order price agreed with the buyer. The borrower is therefore price-agnostic on gold: a rise in gold price increases both the cost of gold received and the export realisation, maintaining the spread.
SEBI-registered MCX Hedge — additional protection: The borrower maintains an active SEBI-registered MCX commodity hedge for anticipated gold purchases exceeding 3 months forward. As on date, the borrower holds MCX sell positions for 8 kg gold at Rs. 72,400/10g, providing downside protection for the next quarter's production schedule. The hedge position and SEBI registration are enclosed.
FinsightOne View on Gold Price Risk: The GML is a pass-through mechanism — not a speculative gold position. The bank's exposure is to the borrower's manufacturing creditworthiness, not to gold price direction. Gold price risk on the bank's collateral (stock of gold) is mitigated by the conservative 75% LTV and monthly stock revaluation at IBJA prices. If gold prices rise, collateral value rises. If gold prices fall, the GML limit is proportionally adjusted via the stock audit mechanism. The risk is managed by structure, not by assumption.

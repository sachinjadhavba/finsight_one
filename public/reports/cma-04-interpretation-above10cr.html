Complex Profile — Group Entity CMA Consolidation & Mid-Cycle Capex Timing Risk
This CMA involves two structural complexities requiring dedicated lender communication: (1) Group entity structure — three inter-related entities (one Pvt Ltd, one partnership, one proprietorship) with cross-guarantees and shared promoters. Standard CMA templates capture standalone financials; FinsightOne's interpretation consolidates group exposure and maps inter-company flows transparently. (2) Mid-cycle capex — the proposed Rs.9.00 Cr TL funds a spinning capacity expansion mid-way through a prior TL repayment cycle. The combined debt service peak in FY26 creates a DSCR trough that must be contextualised with project revenue ramp-up. Both challenges are documentable and fully addressed in this report.

Section 05
CMA Gap Analysis — Identified Issues & FinsightOne Positions
Gap 01 — FY26 DSCR Trough (1.61x) — Mid-Capex Debt Service Peak
High
Observation

In FY26, the company will service both the existing TL (Rs.72L annual, final year) and the proposed TL (Rs.108L annual, commencing year). Combined debt service of Rs.180L against projected cash accruals of Rs.318L gives DSCR of 1.61x — below the 2.0x threshold preferred for project lending above Rs.10 Cr. This trough is mechanically inevitable and is not a deterioration in business performance.

Context

FY26 is the final year of the existing TL (Rs.72L repayment) coinciding with the first full year of the proposed TL (Rs.108L repayment). Revenue from the new capacity begins only in Q2 FY26 (equipment delivery + commissioning: ~6 months from sanction). The revenue ramp from the incremental capacity — Rs.520L incremental turnover — contributes only partially in FY26 but fully from FY27. The DSCR trough is a timing overlap, not a capability deficit.

Mitigant

FinsightOne proposes a 6-month principal moratorium on the proposed TL to avoid FY26 overlap. With moratorium: FY26 debt service = Rs.72L (existing TL only) + Rs.54L (interest only on new TL) = Rs.126L. FY26 DSCR with moratorium = 2.52x — comfortably above threshold. From FY27, existing TL discharged; new TL only = Rs.108L. FY27 DSCR projected 3.41x. Moratorium interest cost is modest (~Rs.27L total) and improves credit quality substantially.

FinsightOne View

Resolvable via moratorium structure. FinsightOne has structured the repayment schedule with a 6-month principal moratorium as the proposed term — not as a workaround but as the appropriate structure for any capex loan with 6-month commissioning lead time. This is standard practice for project term loans. With moratorium, FY26 DSCR exceeds 2.0x and the gap disappears entirely.

Gap 02 — Group TOL/TNW 3.12x — Elevated Consolidated Leverage
Medium
Observation

Consolidated group TOL/TNW of 3.12x exceeds the 3.0x threshold applied by most public sector banks for term lending above Rs.10 Cr. This arises because the partnership and proprietorship entities carry higher leverage ratios (4.2x and 3.8x respectively) relative to the lead entity (2.58x) — pulling up the consolidated ratio.

Context

The partnership (Kalyani Weavers) and proprietorship (Kalyani Exports) are capital-light businesses — weaving and export commission. Their balance sheets carry minimal fixed assets with working capital funded through bank CC. The high TOL/TNW in these entities reflects WC-intensive business models, not structural over-leverage. Their debt is 100% self-liquidating WC CC — no long-term debt, no project loans, no unsecured borrowing.

Mitigant

Lead entity standalone TOL/TNW of 2.58x is within threshold. FinsightOne's approach: (a) present lead entity CMA as the primary credit story, (b) disclose group entities as co-obligants with cross-guarantees (not co-borrowers), (c) show that group entities carry zero long-term debt — their contribution to consolidated TOL is exclusively WC CC which is self-liquidating. Lender exposure is to lead entity; group exposure is disclosed as contingent.

FinsightOne View

Manageable with correct entity structuring. The loan application is structured on lead entity standalone financials. Group entities are disclosed as related parties with guarantees — a transparency measure, not a red flag. Lenders evaluating the lead entity will see 2.58x TOL/TNW (declining trend). The consolidated 3.12x is a mathematical artefact of aggregating WC-funded capital-light businesses — not a signal of group-level over-indebtedness.

Gap 03 — Debtor Days 58 — Moderate Receivables in Export-Adjacent Business
Low
Observation

Debtor days of 58 (FY25), while improving year-on-year (68→62→58), is on the higher end of the range for a domestic textile manufacturer. Some lenders set a 45-day debtor ceiling for manufacturing credit without specific justification — this profile exceeds that ceiling.

Context

The company's top-3 buyers are: an export house (net-60 terms standard in export trade), a state handloom cooperative (government procurement, inherent payment cycle lag), and a hosiery manufacturer (established 7-year relationship, occasional 45–75 day settlements). None of these are high-risk debtors — the extended debtor days reflect buyer profile, not collection risk. Debtor days are improving 10 days/year — directionally correct.

Mitigant

Ageing analysis (annexure): 100% of debtors are within 90 days; zero beyond 120 days; zero disputed. Top-3 debtors represent 72% of total debtors — all with documented purchase agreements and 7+ year payment history. No bad debts written off in 5 years. CIBIL commercial report on top-3 buyers enclosed — all investment-grade. FinsightOne has proposed debtor assignment as a structural enhancement to the CC facility.

FinsightOne View

Low-risk gap with improving trend. 58-day debtors with zero overdue, zero write-offs, and improving annual trajectory is a very low-risk receivables position. The buyer profile (export house + state cooperative + established hoisery manufacturer) justifies the extended terms. This gap is fully documented and is expected to close to 48 days by FY27 per projection — which will be within most lender thresholds without any structural change.

Overall CMA Assessment — FinsightOne Position
This is a complex but fundamentally strong credit profile. The group's 16-year operating history, Rs.4,440 Lakh consolidated turnover, consistent EBITDA margins of 13–14%, and clean CIBIL across promoters and entities form a solid credit foundation. Three gaps identified: DSCR trough (resolved via moratorium structure), consolidated TOL/TNW (resolved via entity structuring), and debtor days (resolved via ageing analysis + buyer credit quality). All three gaps are addressed. FinsightOne's assessment: approvable with 6-month moratorium and lead entity application structure. Recommended lender tier: Mid-size PSU banks (Union Bank, Bank of Baroda) or private banks with textile vertical (Axis Bank, ICICI Commercial).

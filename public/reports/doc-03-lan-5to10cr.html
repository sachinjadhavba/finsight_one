04 INDUSTRY CONTEXT & SECTOR CHARACTERISTICS

Road Construction — Government Sector in India: India's road construction sector is one of the largest recipients of government capital expenditure. Under the PMGSY programme alone, the central government has allocated over Rs. 1.25 lakh crore for rural road connectivity since inception. Maharashtra's PWD annual capex budget exceeds Rs. 8,000 Crore. This ensures a deep and consistent pipeline of work orders for empanelled contractors, with no demand risk.

Payment Cycle — A Sector-Wide Structural Characteristic: The most misunderstood aspect of government contractor lending is the receivables cycle. Government clients do not default — they delay. The payment cycle for PMGSY and PWD Maharashtra works follows a defined administrative process: work completion → Engineer certification → Division Office approval → Treasury release → Bank credit. This cycle typically takes 90 to 180 days. The delay is bureaucratic, not credit-related. Lenders who understand this sector treat 120-day government receivables very differently from 120-day private trade receivables — one is a timing issue, the other is a credit issue.

FinsightOne View on Sector Risk: The primary risk in government contractor lending is not receivable recovery — it is contractor execution capacity. A contractor who cannot execute becomes a contractor who cannot bill, and the government's payment cycle never starts. This borrower's 14-year track record of zero abandonment, with 6 active contracts currently in execution, demonstrates the risk that matters most is well-managed. The elevated debtor days are a feature of the sector, not a characteristic of this borrower's credit quality.

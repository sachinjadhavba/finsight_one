01 EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
This Loan Appraisal Note has been prepared by FinsightOne on behalf of the borrower to present a structured case for a Term Loan of Rs. 1.50 Crore for acquisition of rice processing machinery. The borrower is a well-established partnership firm engaged in rice milling and agro by-product processing at Nellore, Andhra Pradesh, with 12 years of continuous operations. The proposed facility will fund the purchase of a rice sorting & grading machine and paddy dryer, enabling a capacity expansion from 4 MT/hour to 8 MT/hour.
The borrower presents a clean credit bureau profile (partners' CIBIL 748 and 731), consistent revenue growth at approximately 11–13% per annum over three audited years, and a banking turnover of Rs. 3.38 Crore in FY25 — providing strong banking trail relative to the proposed loan. The firm's existing Working Capital facility of Rs. 35 Lakhs is being serviced without overdue. The proposed expansion is backed by confirmed procurement interest from state government agencies and is expected to improve realisations by 30–35% through reduced rejection losses.
The security package proposed — primary hypothecation of funded machinery and equitable mortgage of factory land and building valued at Rs. 2.40 Crore — provides a combined coverage of approximately 293% of the proposed loan amount, offering strong recovery comfort to the lending institution.

04 INDUSTRY & MARKET CONTEXT
Rice milling is a core segment of India's agro-processing value chain. Andhra Pradesh is among the top three paddy-producing states, ensuring consistent raw material availability for the borrower. The regulated procurement ecosystem — FCI, MARKFED and state agencies — provides a price floor that reduces revenue volatility for millers meeting quality standards, which the proposed machinery expansion directly addresses.
The by-product segments (husk fuel blocks and rice bran) represent meaningful incremental revenue — bran constitutes 8–10% of paddy weight and bran oil extraction adds Rs. 12–15 per kg in realisation. The borrower is already monetising these streams and the capacity expansion strengthens the primary milling volume that feeds into them.
Key Risk — Seasonality: Paddy procurement is concentrated in Oct–Dec post-harvest. This is a structural feature of the industry, not a business-specific concern. The borrower manages this through advance procurement contracts with FPOs at fixed prices, reducing spot exposure. The existing WC CC facility absorbs seasonal working capital gaps — this has been serviced without overdue through multiple cycles.

Areas for Lender's Consideration
Partnership constitution: Succession risk in case of incapacitation of lead partner. Both partners are active in the business; business continuity insurance is proposed as a safeguard.
Seasonal revenue concentration: Oct–Dec procurement-heavy pattern is an industry-wide characteristic, not business-specific. WC CC facility has historically absorbed lean period gaps without overdue.
TOL/TNW rises to ~2.48x post-loan from current 1.06x — remains well within the 4x outer norm. Will revert as loan is progressively repaid over 60 months.
Single commodity concentration (rice): Partially offset by by-product revenue streams (husk blocks, bran) which provide year-round cash flow independent of main milling volumes.

02 INCOME CONSISTENCY AUDIT — ITR vs GST vs BANKING
The income consistency audit compares three independent data sources — ITR (declared income), GST GSTR-1 (sales declared to tax authorities), and banking (actual credits in current account). Note 1 — ITR vs GST Minor Divergence (Rs. 11–16 Lakhs)
The small divergence between ITR gross sales and GST taxable turnover is attributable to: (a) medicines under Schedule H and H1 are exempt from GST — these are included in ITR sales but do not appear in GSTR-1 taxable turnover; (b) credit notes and returns post-period which adjust GST filings but not ITR. This is a standard feature of pharmaceutical distribution. FinsightOne View: No concern.
Note 2 — Banking Turnover 72% of ITR (KEY GAP)
The banking/ITR ratio of 0.719x — consistent across 3 years — is the primary gap in this file. Observation: Banking credits are approximately 28% lower than ITR-declared sales. Context: Pharmaceutical distribution involves payment by retailers in mixed modes — approximately 60% RTGS/NEFT (captured in banking), 28% post-dated cheques deposited in personal savings account (not in business CC), and 12% cash (declared in ITR, not banking). This is sector-standard for Tier-2 distributor collections. Mitigant: Borrower has agreed to migrate 100% of collections to the CC account going forward as a condition of enhancement. GST/ITR alignment at 98.6% confirms there is no undeclared income — the gap is a collections routing issue, not an income fabrication issue. FinsightOne View: Acceptable with collection routing undertaking. GST alignment provides the critical income verification that banking alone cannot provide here.

05 GAPS & WEAKNESSES — IDENTIFIED, CONTEXTUALISED, MITIGATED
GAP 1 — Banking Turnover at 72% of ITR (Consistent 3-Year Pattern)
High Priority
OBSERVATION

Banking credits in CC + CA account total Rs. 808 Lakhs in FY25 against ITR-declared sales of Rs. 1,124 Lakhs. The shortfall of Rs. 316 Lakhs (28%) has been consistent at ~28% across all three audited years — ruling out a one-time anomaly.

CONTEXT

Pharmaceutical retail collections in Tier-2 India routinely involve post-dated cheques deposited in the proprietor's personal savings account (a common practice among smaller distributors) and cash collections for smaller pharmacies. Neither route reflects in the CC account. The GST/ITR alignment at 98.6% rules out income fabrication — the sales are real and declared to tax authorities; only the collection route differs.

MITIGANT

Structural fix proposed: (1) Borrower to provide undertaking to route 100% collections through CC account within 90 days of enhancement. (2) Bank to verify personal savings account statements for FY25 — expected to show Rs. 280–320 Lakhs credits matching the gap. (3) Future renewal to be conditioned on banking turnover reaching 90%+ of GST turnover.

FINSIGHTONE VIEW

Acceptable with routing undertaking. This is a documentation and banking behaviour issue, not an income fabrication issue. GST provides the independent income verification that banking alone cannot provide in this case. Residual risk: MEDIUM → LOW with undertaking.

GAP 2 — Debtor Days at 62 Days (Above 30–45 Day Trading Norm)
Medium Priority
OBSERVATION

Debtor days of 62 days are above the 30–45 day norm expected for trading businesses. Outstanding debtors at Rs. 190.8 Lakhs represent 17% of annual revenue — a significant proportion of current assets tied up in receivables.

CONTEXT

Pharmaceutical distribution to independent pharmacies (which constitute 65% of this borrower's client base) consistently runs at 45–75 days in Tier-2 markets. This is a sector characteristic. The borrower's top 20 clients account for 62% of revenue — all are established pharmacies with 3–8 year relationships and no default history.

MITIGANT

Debtor ageing statement enclosed — zero debtors beyond 90 days. Zero bad debt write-off in the last 3 years. CC sizing has been calibrated to the 62-day cycle with 26% headroom (CC Rs. 140L vs WC requirement Rs. 111L). Improving trend (68→62 days) demonstrates active collection management.

FINSIGHTONE VIEW

Acceptable — within sector norm for this client mix. The combination of zero bad debts, improving trend, and CC sized to cover the cycle makes this a monitored parameter, not a disqualifying concern. Residual risk: LOW.

GAP 3 — Thin EBITDA Margin (3.87% on Sales)
Low Priority
OBSERVATION

EBITDA margin of 3.87% on net sales is thin. A revenue decline of 41% is required to break even on CC interest — indicating low margin of safety relative to revenue volatility.

CONTEXT

Pharmaceutical distribution margins in India range from 6–10% gross margin with 3–5% EBITDA — this borrower's margins are within sector norms. The absolute EBITDA of Rs. 43.54 Lakhs in FY25 is sufficient for business needs. Pharma distribution revenue is highly resilient — essential medicine demand is inelastic and volumes are stable regardless of economic cycles.

MITIGANT

Revenue resilience of pharma distribution mitigates the margin thinness. 6-year track record with zero revenue decline in any year. CC interest coverage of 3.61x in base case provides 2.61x downside before debt service is threatened. The business has never had a year where EBITDA did not cover its debt obligations.

FINSIGHTONE VIEW

No concern at this loan size. Thin margins are sector-standard. The relevant risk is revenue stability — pharma distribution revenue is among the most stable revenue streams in MSME lending. Residual risk: LOW.

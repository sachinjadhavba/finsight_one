Section 05
CMA Gap Analysis — Identified Issues & FinsightOne Positions
Gap 01 — Seasonal Revenue Creates Misleading Annualised Ratios
High
Observation

Q1 FY25 standalone revenue of Rs.22.5 Lakhs annualises to Rs.90 Lakhs — 12% of full-year actuals. If a banker screens the Q1 quarterly cash flows in isolation, the annualised DSCR drops to 1.42x and TOL/TNW appears elevated relative to operating capacity. Standard CMA templates do not flag this as a seasonal pattern — they present it as a cash flow weakness.

Context

Cold storage businesses globally operate on harvest-cycle revenue patterns. Maharashtra's Rabi calendar (Nov–Mar dominant) is published by the State Agriculture Department and is consistent across the sector. This is not a business-specific weakness — it is how the sector works. Banks with cold chain experience (SBI, Bank of Maharashtra, NABARD-refinanced lenders) routinely evaluate annual DSCR, not quarterly annualised DSCR, for seasonal agricultural businesses.

Mitigant

FinsightOne has appended a quarterly revenue mapping (Section 02) showing the seasonal profile across FY23–FY25 with Rabi/Kharif cycle overlay. Full-year DSCR of 2.34x (FY25) comfortably covers debt service including proposed TL EMI. Annual cash accruals of Rs.120 Lakhs against annual debt service of Rs.51 Lakhs (proposed) = coverage ratio of 2.35x. The lean period is absorbed within the annual cycle without CC overdue.

FinsightOne View

This gap is resolvable through lender selection and narrative. Lenders with NABARD Agri-allied vertical exposure understand harvest-cycle businesses. FinsightOne's lender shortlisting for this profile excludes lenders who evaluate seasonal businesses on quarterly cash flow screens. With appropriate lender routing, this gap does not arise at the credit desk.

Gap 02 — High Inventory Days (158 Days) — Structural vs. Liquidity Signal
Medium
Observation

Inventory days of 158 in FY25 will flag amber or red in most automated credit scoring systems. Standard CMA ratio interpretation treats high inventory days as a sign of slow-moving stock or collection difficulty — which would represent a genuine credit risk in a trading or manufacturing context.

Context

In cold storage, inventory IS the service product. The company doesn't own the commodity — it stores it on behalf of farmers and traders. Inventory on the balance sheet represents stored goods under contractual obligation, not unsold product risk. The higher the storage utilisation, the higher the inventory days — which is a revenue signal, not a risk signal. Inventory in cold chain = revenue backlog.

Mitigant

Storage contracts (presented in annexure) confirm 92% utilisation as of report date. All stored commodity is either owned by the borrower (Rs.220L, margin-funded) or held on lien/pledge for trade clients (Rs.218L, covered by bailment receipts). No speculative or uncovered stock. Insurance coverage of Rs.480 Lakhs active — exceeds total stock value.

FinsightOne View

Resolvable with sector context note. FinsightOne has drafted a sector context paragraph (included in loan proposal) explaining cold chain inventory mechanics to the credit appraiser. Once the banker understands that 158-day inventory = 92% facility utilisation (a positive metric), the ratio is reinterpreted correctly. This is a documentation gap, not a financial weakness.

Gap 03 — Banking / ITR Variance — ~5% Unexplained Gap
Low
Observation

Banking turnover across FY23–FY25 reflects 94–96% of ITR declared sales — a 4–6% gap exists consistently. While this is significantly better than the 70–80% gaps seen in trading profiles, some banks flag any banking/ITR divergence as a potential income understatement signal.

Context

The gap is attributable to a specific, documentable transaction type: advance storage deposits received in cash from small farmers. Maharashtra's agricultural communities — particularly Nashik district onion growers — often pay cold storage booking advances in cash (Rs.500–2,000 per tonne). These are declared in ITR as service income but settle as cash, not banking credit. This is a documented, legal, sector-specific cash flow pattern.

Mitigant

FinsightOne has prepared a cash flow reconciliation note matching the Rs.36 Lakhs (FY25) banking/ITR gap to farmer advance receipts, supported by: (a) farm-level mandi receipts confirming farmer identities, (b) GST-compliant tax invoices for all storage services, (c) ITR Schedule BP reflecting service income inclusive of cash advances. No income suppression — full transparency.

FinsightOne View

Fully resolvable — documentation is complete. A 94–96% banking/ITR ratio is among the strongest seen in agricultural service businesses. The reconciliation note pre-empts the lender's query and demonstrates that the company maintains transparent books. This gap, once explained, typically converts to a credit strength (disciplined income reporting).

Root Cause Analysis — 4 Reasons Your Application Was Declined
📉
Cause 1 — CIBIL Score Below Floor
Score: 698 vs Bank's 700 minimum
Critical
Your CIBIL score at the time of application was 698 — 2 points below this bank's stated minimum of 700 for home loans to self-employed borrowers. The score was pulled down primarily by 3 hard enquiries in 6 months (this bank + 2 earlier lenders you spoke to informally).
How the lender saw it: "Score below product floor. Ineligible for processing under current credit policy. No exceptions applicable for self-employed applicants below 700."
🔧
Fix: Stop all new credit applications immediately. Hard enquiries age and reduce impact after 6 months. Pay any outstanding credit card dues fully. Score will naturally recover to 715–720 within 90 days of no new enquiries — then apply.
📊
Cause 2 — Income Inconsistency (ITR)
FY24: Rs.3.8L vs FY25: Rs.7.2L declared
Critical
ITR shows declared net income of Rs.3.8L in FY24 jumping to Rs.7.2L in FY25 — a 90% increase in one year. For self-employed IT consultants, lenders view sudden income spikes with suspicion. Most lenders average the last 2–3 years and use the lower or average figure for eligibility calculation.
How the lender saw it: "Income appears inflated in latest year to meet eligibility. Using 2-year average of Rs.5.5L/yr. Eligible amount at this income level: Rs.38–40L — applicant has applied for Rs.55L. Eligibility gap exists."
🔧
Fix: File FY26 ITR showing consistent income of Rs.7L+ before reapplying. Two years of Rs.7L+ removes the spike concern — lenders see a trend, not a jump. FY26 ITR filing is the single most important action to take.
⚖️
Cause 3 — FOIR Threshold Exceeded
Post-loan FOIR: 82% vs Bank's 75% cap
Major
Using the bank's averaged income figure of Rs.5.5L/year (Rs.45,833/month), and your existing obligations (personal loan EMI Rs.12,000 + credit card minimum Rs.3,000), the proposed home loan EMI of Rs.43,500/month pushes your total FOIR to 82% — above this bank's 75% ceiling for self-employed borrowers. Note: different banks have different FOIR caps (some go to 80%+); this bank's policy was restrictive.
How the lender saw it: "Post-obligation FOIR at 82% exceeds our 75% policy limit for SE borrowers. Under a co-applicant structure, this may be reassessable — but no co-applicant was included in this application."
🔧
Fix: Pre-close your personal loan (outstanding ~Rs.1.8L — manageable) before reapplying. This removes Rs.12,000/month from obligations, bringing FOIR to ~70%. Alternatively, add spouse as co-applicant if they have income — income clubbing improves eligible amount significantly.
🔎
Cause 4 — Multiple Hard Enquiries
3 enquiries in 6 months — bureau red flag
Major
Your CIBIL report shows 3 hard enquiries in the past 6 months — one from the rejected application above, and two from when you spoke to other lenders/DSAs earlier this year and they pulled your bureau without your complete awareness. Multiple enquiries signal "credit-hungry" behaviour to lenders and are a standalone negative signal beyond the score impact.
How the lender saw it: "Applicant has 3 bureau pulls in 6 months. Pattern suggests the applicant was rejected or declined informally at 2 prior institutions. This is a cautionary signal for credit discipline."
🔧
Fix: Do not give consent to any lender or DSA/broker to pull your CIBIL for the next 90 days. Always ask: "Is this a soft check or hard enquiry?" Only consent to a hard enquiry when you are fully ready to proceed with that specific lender. Use FinsightOne's soft assessment first.

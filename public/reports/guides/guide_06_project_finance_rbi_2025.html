RBI's 2025 Project Finance Directions:
What Borrowers Need to Know

The new mandatory DPR and DSCR requirements and how they change what banks need before sanctioning project loans. If you're planning infrastructure, manufacturing, or renewable energy financing in 2025 — read this first.

📖 8 min read
👤 For: Infrastructure, manufacturing, real estate, renewable energy borrowers
⚖️ Based on RBI Master Directions 2025
What Changed in 2025 — The Quick Summary

RBI's Master Directions on Project Finance Loans were revised significantly with effect from FY2025. After a period of consultation and delay, the final guidelines were notified, introducing stricter pre-sanction requirements and clearer NPA classification rules for project finance. If you have applied for or are planning to apply for a project loan above ₹25 Crore, these changes directly affect your application.

Reference Circular: RBI Master Directions — Prudential Framework for Project Finance (2025), issued under Section 21 read with Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Applicable to all Scheduled Commercial Banks, NBFCs-ML and above, and HFCs for loans classified as "project finance." Effective: October 2025 (post-transition period).
Key Changes and What They Mean for You
1. Mandatory Detailed Project Report (DPR) — Now Stricter
❌ Old: Banks could rely on a promoter-prepared feasibility report for smaller project loans. DPR standards were not prescribed in detail.
✓ New: For project loans above ₹25 Crore, banks must obtain a DPR prepared by or reviewed by an independent technical consultant empanelled with the bank. The DPR must address: project viability, technology risk, demand study, cost of project with quantity takeoffs, schedule, and risk analysis.

What this means for you: If you submit a DPR prepared only by your own team, banks must now have it independently reviewed. Factor in 2–4 weeks additional time and approximately ₹50,000–₹2 lakh cost for technical due diligence.

2. Stricter DSCR Requirements During Construction Period
❌ Old: No specific minimum DSCR was prescribed during the moratorium/construction period. Banks had discretion.
✓ New: Minimum Average DSCR of 1.20x over the operating life of the project (not just the repayment period). In "stressed" periods (Year 1–2 of operations), minimum 1.10x is acceptable with documentation of recovery trajectory.

What this means for you: Your financial projections must show DSCR ≥ 1.20x on average. Borderline cases (1.10–1.19x) will face heightened scrutiny and may require additional comfort measures (debt service reserve account, sponsor support letter).

3. New NPA Classification Rules — Project Loans Get Extra Time
❌ Old: A project loan could be classified NPA after 90 days of default, even during construction phase — creating unfair NPA classification for genuine construction delays.
✓ New: For projects under implementation, the NPA clock starts only after the commercial operations date (COD) + a reasonable stress period. Pre-COD delays of up to 3 years (from original COD) can be accommodated via restructuring without NPA classification, subject to conditions (bank board approval, continued sponsor support).

What this means for you: Genuine project delays due to land acquisition, regulatory approvals, or force majeure will be treated with more flexibility. However, the promoter must remain financially supportive and the delay must be documented with a revised COD estimate.

4. Mandatory Debt Service Reserve Account (DSRA)
❌ Old: DSRA was a common requirement but not universally mandated. Some banks accepted a DSRA equivalent letter of comfort from promoter.
✓ New: Projects above ₹100 Crore must maintain a DSRA equivalent to minimum 3–6 months of debt service (principal + interest). The DSRA must be funded before first drawdown.

What this means for you: If your project cost is ₹100+ Crore, you need to budget for DSRA upfront. On a ₹100 Crore project, 6 months of debt service might be ₹5–8 Crore that needs to be parked in a separate escrow account. Build this into your means of finance.

5. Environmental & Social Risk Assessment — Now Mandatory
❌ Old: E&S assessment required only for large infrastructure projects. Most industrial loans did not require a standalone E&S report.
✓ New: For project loans above ₹25 Crore, banks must conduct an Environmental and Social Risk Assessment (ESRA) as part of pre-sanction due diligence. This covers: pollution clearance, land acquisition status, environmental impact, social displacement (if any), and local regulatory compliance.

What this means for you: Obtain your Consent to Establish (CTE) and Consent to Operate (CTO) from the State Pollution Control Board before applying. Banks will not sanction without confirmed environmental clearances for applicable categories.

What You Should Do Before Applying for Project Finance in 2025
  • Commission an independent DPR from a reputable technical consultant — not your in-house team alone
  • Ensure your financial model shows DSCR ≥ 1.20x on average, with sensitivity analysis for downside scenarios
  • Get environmental clearances before approaching banks — the process takes 2–6 months
  • Budget for DSRA in your means of finance if the project is above ₹100 Crore
  • Apply to banks that have demonstrated appetite for your sector — check their annual report disclosures or sector-wise credit data
  • For consortium loans, identify the lead bank first and get their in-principle approval before approaching member banks
Pro Tip: The new 2025 guidelines are significantly more detailed than what banks were working with before. Many bank credit officers are themselves still learning the new requirements. If you have a project finance application, consider working with an advisor who understands the new framework — a file prepared to the new standard will sail through credit committees faster than one that has to be sent back for additional information repeatedly.

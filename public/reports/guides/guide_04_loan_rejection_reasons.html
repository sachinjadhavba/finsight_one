🔄 Loan Rejection

Why Banks Reject Loan Applications
(And How to Fix Each Reason)

The 8 most common rejection reasons — and the exact fixes that get files re-submitted and approved. Written from the other side of the desk, by ex-bank credit officers.

📖 9 min read
👤 For: Anyone who has been rejected or wants to avoid rejection
🔧 Each reason includes a specific fix
What Banks Cannot Tell You — But We Can

When a bank rejects your loan, RBI's Fair Practices Code requires them to communicate the reasons in writing. In practice, rejection letters are vague — "does not meet our credit criteria" is technically compliant but tells you nothing actionable.

Your Right Under RBI Guidelines: Under RBI's Fair Practices Code for Lenders (Master Circular RBI/2015-16/103), banks must give you a written rejection with specific reasons within a reasonable time. If your rejection letter is vague, you can formally request a detailed explanation. Most banks will provide it — especially for MSME loans under Samadhaan portal guidelines.
The 8 Most Common Rejection Reasons
1
Low CIBIL Score — Personal or Company
Most Frequent
Most PSU banks require a minimum CIBIL score of 700+ for business loans. Private banks often set the bar at 720–750+. A score below this triggers automatic decline in their credit scoring systems — the credit officer may never even review the file.
✓ The Fix

Check your report for errors first (wrong entries account for many low scores). Then: reduce credit card utilisation below 30%, resolve any outstanding missed payments, and wait 3–6 months before reapplying. Alternatively, apply to a lender whose minimum score threshold is lower (NBFC, co-operative bank, or a PSU bank with MSME-specific programs). See our CIBIL Guide for the full improvement roadmap.

2
Insufficient Income / DSCR Below Bank Benchmark
Very Frequent
For business loans, banks compute a Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) — how many times your net cash flow covers the proposed EMI or annual debt obligations. Banks typically require minimum 1.25x average DSCR over the loan tenure. If your CA's projections show DSCR below this, the loan gets declined even if the business is genuinely profitable.
✓ The Fix

Check: (a) Is the DSCR being calculated correctly — is all income included? Many CAs miss PPA income, rent income, or subsidy income. (b) Is the depreciation method consistent? (c) Can you increase the loan tenure to reduce annual debt service? A 7-year loan has smaller annual repayment than a 5-year loan — improving DSCR without changing your income. (d) Can you bring in additional income through a co-borrower or guarantor?

3
Insufficient Collateral / Security Coverage
Very Frequent
Banks typically require collateral (security) covering 100–150% of the loan amount. If the valuation of your property or assets falls short, they decline or reduce the loan. Common problems: outdated property valuations, wrong FSV (Forced Sale Value) methodology applied, commercial/industrial property being valued using residential comparables.
✓ The Fix

Get an independent revaluation from an RBI-approved valuer who specialises in your asset type (industrial property, fleet, machinery). Banks cannot legally insist on using only their empanelled valuer for the initial assessment. Also check: CGTMSE (Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises) covers loans up to ₹5 Crore without collateral — many MSME borrowers don't know they qualify.

CGTMSE: RBI-backed scheme allowing MSMEs to get loans without collateral up to ₹5 Crore. Lenders bear risk under the guarantee. Ask specifically about "CGTMSE coverage" when rejected for collateral shortfall.
4
High Existing Debt / Leverage — TOL/TNW Above Bank Limit
Frequent
Banks compute Total Outside Liabilities / Tangible Net Worth (TOL/TNW). If your total debt (including the proposed loan) divided by your net worth exceeds 3:1 or 4:1 (varies by bank), you're considered over-leveraged. This is especially common when businesses have been growing fast through debt.
✓ The Fix

Two levers: (a) Increase net worth — promoter equity infusion, retained profits, preference share issue. Even ₹25–50 lakh infusion can dramatically change the ratio. (b) Reduce outside liabilities — close or prepay some existing loans, or convert short-term liabilities to long-term (reclassifying payables improves the ratio). Prepare a notarised undertaking for equity infusion as a condition attached to sanction.

5
Poor GST Return Filing / ITR–GST Mismatch
Frequent
Banks now routinely cross-check GST GSTR-1 (sales), GSTR-3B (net tax), and ITR filed income. If GST shows ₹8 Crore turnover but ITR declares ₹4 Crore, it's an immediate red flag — it implies either tax evasion or data entry errors. Either way, the credit officer flags it and the file goes to audit or gets rejected.
✓ The Fix

If the mismatch is genuine (e.g., exports not reflected in taxable GST, inter-branch transfers inflating GST numbers), prepare a CA-certified reconciliation note explaining the difference line by line before submitting the application. Do not hope the bank doesn't notice — they always do now that GSTN data is available directly.

6
Business Too New — Less Than 3 Years Vintage
Common for New Businesses
Most banks require a business to be at least 2–3 years old with audited financials for those years before they consider a business loan. Startups and companies incorporated in the last 24 months face systemic rejection from traditional banks.
✓ The Fix

For newer businesses: (a) Apply under a government scheme — Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana (for loans up to ₹10 lakh), Stand-Up India scheme, or Startup India credit guarantee. (b) Apply for a secured OD against property instead of a business loan. (c) Approach NBFCs who have lower vintage requirements. (d) If the promoter has prior business experience, document it — banks treat "business age" more leniently when promoters have strong track records.

7
Existing NPA / Loan Default — SMA-2 or Worse
Difficult but Addressable
If any existing loan account has been classified as Special Mention Account (SMA-1, SMA-2) or Non-Performing Asset (NPA), virtually all banks will decline new credit facilities. This includes defaults at any bank — the information is shared across the system via CIBIL and RBI's CRILC database.
✓ The Fix

There is no shortcut here. The sequence is: (1) Regularise the defaulted account — pay all overdue EMIs and charges. (2) Request the bank to upgrade the account from NPA/SMA to standard. (3) Wait 6–12 months of clean repayment. (4) Then apply for new credit. Alternatively, under RBI's OTS (One Time Settlement) guidelines, some accounts can be settled and the record upgraded — this requires negotiation with the lender and takes 3–6 months. FinsightOne can assist with bank negotiation for NPA resolution.

8
Wrong Bank or Wrong Product — Mismatch of Need and Product
Underappreciated
Many rejections happen because the borrower applied to the wrong bank (one that doesn't lend to their sector or size), or applied for the wrong product (applied for CC when they needed a TL, applied for a term loan when they needed working capital). Banks have internal sector-wise credit policies and risk appetite that change periodically — a bank that sanctioned a hotel loan last year may have placed hotels on their "negative list" this year.
✓ The Fix

Before applying: (a) Check the bank's recent sector lending activity — most PSU banks publish quarterly sectoral exposure on their websites. (b) Talk to the branch manager before submitting the formal application — an informal discussion often reveals whether the branch has appetite. (c) Consider alternate lenders: NBFCs, small finance banks, and co-operative banks often have different sector preferences and risk appetite from large PSU banks.

How to Improve Your CIBIL Score
Before Applying for a Loan

Six steps that genuinely move the needle — and why closing old credit cards can actually hurt your score. Common myths debunked by our ex-bank credit officers.

📖 7 min read
👤 For: Business owners + Individuals planning a loan
⏱️ Results: 3–6 months
What CIBIL Score Do Banks Want?

Banks use your CIBIL score (TransUnion CIBIL Score ranges from 300 to 900) as the first gate. Here's what each range means in practice:

300–579
Very Poor
Most banks decline automatically
580–649
Poor
High-risk category; very limited lenders
650–699
Fair
Some banks, higher interest rate
700–749
Good
Most banks; standard rate
750+
Excellent
Best rates; preferred customer
For MSME / Business Loans: If the company's CIBIL Commercial Rank (CMR) is poor but the promoter's personal CIBIL is 750+, many banks will process the loan primarily based on the promoter's personal score — especially for loans below ₹5 Crore. The reverse is also true: a company with a good track record can sometimes overcome a promoter's average personal score.
6 Steps That Actually Improve Your Score
1
Never Miss an EMI — Not Even One Highest Impact

Payment history accounts for approximately 35% of your CIBIL score. A single missed EMI — even by 1 day — can drop your score by 50–80 points and stays on your report for 7 years. The fix: set up ECS auto-debit for every loan EMI and credit card due. Do it today, before you forget.

If you've already missed payments, the path is straightforward but slow: pay the outstanding immediately, get a "No Due Certificate" from the lender, and then simply wait. Scores begin recovering within 3–6 months of consistent repayment.

⏱️ Impact seen: Immediately upon on-time payment — gradual improvement over 6–12 months
2
Keep Credit Card Utilisation Below 30% High Impact

Credit utilisation — what percentage of your credit limit you're using — is one of the biggest score factors. If your credit card limit is ₹5 lakh and you're regularly using ₹4.5 lakh, you appear "maxed out" to scoring models even if you pay it off every month.

The fix: either reduce your spending to below 30% of the limit, or call your bank and request a limit increase (without actually spending more). A higher limit at the same spending = lower utilisation = better score.

⏱️ Impact seen: In the next billing cycle (30–45 days)
3
Check Your CIBIL Report for Errors High Impact

This step is consistently underestimated. In our experience reviewing credit files, approximately 1 in 4 reports has at least one factual error — a loan that was already closed but still shows as outstanding, a wrong default entry, or a loan belonging to someone with a similar name attached to your profile.

How to check: Go to cibil.com — you get one free report per year. Look for: (1) Loans you don't recognise, (2) "Settled" accounts that should be "Closed", (3) Wrong loan outstanding amounts. File a dispute on the CIBIL website and with the relevant lender simultaneously. Corrections typically take 30–45 days.

⏱️ Impact seen: 30–60 days after error correction
4
Avoid Multiple Loan Applications in a Short Period Medium Impact

Every time a bank pulls your CIBIL (a "hard inquiry"), it slightly reduces your score — by about 5–10 points each time. If you apply to 5 banks in 3 months, that's 5 hard inquiries, and it signals desperation for credit. This is why you should approach only 1–2 banks at a time, not "spray and pray."

Compare loan terms through an advisor or broker (who does a "soft inquiry") rather than by formally applying everywhere.

⏱️ Each hard inquiry recovers in 6–12 months automatically
5
Maintain a Mix of Credit Types Medium Impact

Having only credit cards hurts your score compared to a mix of a home loan (secured, long-term), a personal loan (unsecured, short-term), and a credit card. If you only have one type of credit product, consider adding another — but only if you can genuinely service it.

⏱️ Impact over 12–24 months of good repayment history
6
For Business Loans: Build Your Company's CIBIL Commercial Rank (CMR) Medium Impact for Business

The CIBIL Commercial Rank (CMR-1 to CMR-10, with 1 being best) is specific to companies. It's built by: timely repayment of all existing business loans, maintaining a CC/OD account with regular operation (not just drawdowns), filing GST and ITR consistently, and having a credit history of at least 2 years.

New companies (under 2 years) may not have a CMR — in this case, promoter's personal CIBIL becomes the primary signal for small business loans.

⏱️ CMR builds over 18–36 months of consistent credit behaviour
Common Myths — Busted
❌ MYTH: Closing old credit cards improves your score
✓ FACT: Closing old cards typically hurts your score

Closing an old card reduces your total available credit limit — which automatically increases your utilisation ratio. Older cards also have a longer credit history, which is a positive factor. Unless the card has a very high annual fee that you're losing money on, keep it open and use it occasionally for small purchases.

❌ MYTH: "Settled" on your report is same as "Closed"
✓ FACT: "Settled" is one of the worst statuses on a CIBIL report

"Settled" means you negotiated with the bank to pay less than the full outstanding amount. Banks treat this as a partial default — almost as bad as NPA. If a lender has marked your account as "Settled," try to pay the remaining amount and get it reclassified as "Closed." It can be done — and it matters enormously.

❌ MYTH: Checking your own CIBIL score hurts it
✓ FACT: Checking your own report is a "soft inquiry" — no impact on score

Only "hard inquiries" (when a bank or lender pulls your score for a loan application) impact your score. Checking your own report through cibil.com, Experian, or any consumer-facing portal has zero impact. Check it regularly — at least once every 6 months.
